# Reply to a thread

I paste a thread or a comment from a tracker, chat, or review and want three things in order: what the other person is actually saying, what I think about it, and a reply I can send. The reply is the output; the first two exist so I can check the reply is right.

## Procedure

1. **Read the whole thread, not the last message.** The last comment usually answers something earlier; read from the top. If I pasted a screenshot, transcribe what matters before interpreting it. If the thread references a ticket, PR, or doc I also gave you, read that too. Done when you can say who said what, in order, in one line each.
2. **State what they are saying, in plain words.** Two to four short sentences. Translate jargon, acronyms, and indirect phrasing ("I wonder if we could consider...") into the direct claim or request. If a message is in a language I do not read, translate it first. Mark anything ambiguous as ambiguous instead of picking a reading silently.
3. **State what they want from me.** One line: a decision, an answer, an approval, information, or nothing (an FYI). If nothing, say so; many threads need no reply.
4. **Give my position.** Based on what I said in this conversation and the facts in the thread. Lead with the recommendation, one reason, and what would change it. If the position depends on a decision only I can make, stop here and ask me that one question instead of guessing. A drafted reply that commits me to something I did not decide is worse than no draft.
5. **Draft the reply** using the `proofreading` skill's voice rules and drafting patterns: open with the point, position in first person with soft hedges, one plain reason, what I already did, the ask on its own line. Match the thread's formality and length; a two-line comment gets a two-to-four-line reply.
6. **Return it in this shape:**

   What they are saying: ...
   What they want from me: ...
   My read: ...

   Draft reply:
   (the reply text, ready to paste, no code fence)

## Rules

- Plain English throughout. No restating the thread back to me in full; I have it.
- Do not invent facts, dates, decisions, or commitments. If the reply needs one I did not give, leave a visible gap (`(date TBC)`) or ask.
- Do not overstate evidence. If the thread's claim is one person's observation, say so in my read, and the reply treats it that way.
- Keep names out of my read where a role works ("the reviewer"); in the draft reply, address people the way the thread does.
- If feedback on their comment was requested rather than a reply, give the feedback (what is right, what is missing, what is risky) and skip the draft unless I ask for one.
- If the thread is a decision already made and no one asked me anything, say "no reply needed" and stop.

## Done when

I can read the three lines and confirm the draft says what I would say, the draft contains no commitment I did not make, and it is ready to paste without editing.
