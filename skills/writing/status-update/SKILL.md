# Status update (What / So What / Now What, and PREP)

I bring a ticket, doc, Slack post, or notes and need to present it, usually out loud in a meeting with managers. Two formats, two jobs:

- **What / So What / Now What** reports. The listener learns what happened, why it matters, and what happens next.
- **PREP (Point, Reason, Example, Point)** persuades. Use it when I need a decision, approval, or agreement and expect pushback.

Default to What / So What / Now What. Use PREP only for the one part of the update where I need to win the point, not for the whole thing.

## Procedure

1. **Pick the format.** Ask: is there anything I need them to approve, fund, or agree to against resistance? No → What / So What / Now What. Yes → What / So What / Now What for the update, with PREP for that one ask. If I am only persuading (a proposal, not an update), use PREP alone. Done when you can name the format in one line and why.
2. **Find the headline and the ask.** One line each. Headline = overall status ("on track, one risk", "draft ready, need your confirmation"). Ask = the decision or action I want from them, or "none, FYI". If the material has no clear ask, say so instead of inventing one.
3. **Check how much they already know.** If it was already shared (offline, earlier meeting), it is a reminder plus next step: shorter What, no background, go straight to the ask. If it is new, give one line of context first.
4. **Fill the parts, one to three short sentences each:**
   - **Headline:** status in one line, then the ask preview.
   - **What:** facts only, what was done or what changed. One strong number, not every number.
   - **So What:** what it means for cost, dates, risk, or people. This is my position: "I think ...".
   - **Now What:** next steps with owners, then the ask with a deadline, then "If we are aligned, I'll ...".
   - **PREP (if needed):** Point (my position in one line), Reason (one plain reason), Example (one concrete fact or case), Point (restate as the ask).
5. **Cut for time.** Spoken target is 45 to 60 seconds. Drop reasons lists, approval tables, glossaries, and background; they stay in the linked doc. Keep side topics as one optional line at the end, marked optional.
6. **Return it in this shape:**
   - The talk track with bold labels (**Headline**, **What**, **So What**, **Now What**, plus **PREP** if used), written as quotes I can say.
   - One optional line, if there is a side topic.
   - Up to three short notes: what I cut and where it lives, any gap I must fill, and when to switch to PREP if they push back.
   - If I want something to glance at while presenting, hand the track to the `speaker-mind-map` skill.

## Rules

- Write it to be said out loud: short sentences, one breath each, plain words, no jargon the room would not use.
- Voice follows the `proofreading` skill: first person, soft hedges ("I think", "for now", "if we are aligned"), active voice, commas instead of em dashes.
- Facts come from my material only. Do not invent dates, owners, numbers, or decisions; leave a visible gap (`[date]`, `(owner TBC)`) instead. Keep worst-case numbers labeled as worst case ("up to", "if fully used").
- Every ask has a deadline. If I did not give one, leave `[date]` and flag it; "let me know your concerns" with no date gets no reply.
- No PREP for plain status. PREP on a routine update sounds like I am arguing with them.
- If the material is a written message (Slack, doc), keep links out of the spoken track and tell me to drop them in the meeting chat.

## Example (shape only)

Material: a policy turning off paid overage on a team tool, already discussed offline, needs leads' confirmation.

**Headline:** "Quick update on the overage policy I shared earlier. The draft is ready, and I'd like your confirmation before we share it with everyone."
**What:** "Today all 40 members have paid overage on by default. It's billed at a much higher rate, up to `$9,600/year` if fully used."
**So What:** "I think hitting the limit should make us review how we plan, not add credits. So the policy treats the limit like working hours and overage like overtime."
**Now What:** "Overage is off by default, allowed only for incidents or deadlines at risk, with manager approval. Please check the policy and share concerns by [date]. If we are aligned, I'll roll it out."

## Done when

The format choice is stated, the first line gives the status, the last line gives the ask with a deadline (or a visible `[date]` gap), the track reads aloud in under a minute, and every fact in it came from my material.
