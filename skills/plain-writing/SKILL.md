# Plain writing

Write the way I write: short, direct, plain English with a clear position. The reader should get the point in the first line and the ask in the last.

## Procedure

1. **Find the point and the ask.** Before writing, name in one line what the reader must know and what they must do. If there is no ask, the message is an update; say so in the first line.
2. **Open with the point or the context.** "I checked the details." / "As discussed, ..." / "I would like to check if ...". Never open with praise, thanks for the question, or a restatement of what was asked.
3. **State the position in first person.** "I think ...", "I would like to ...", "Moving forward, ...". Soft hedges are fine ("for now", "if we are aligned"); vague ones are not ("it might be worth considering").
4. **Give one plain reason per decision.** "As X becomes more frequent, it adds Y." Tie it to the goal when the reader may push back.
5. **Say what was already done,** with the link on its own line.
6. **End with the ask on its own line.** "Please confirm and close this if there are no concerns." / "Please let me know your thoughts. If we are aligned, could you ...?"

## Rules

- Short lines, one or two sentences each, blank line between blocks. Paragraphs are 1–3 sentences.
- Simple everyday words. No jargon the reader did not use first.
- Active voice. "Claude syncs it once the PR merges", not "it will be synced once approved".
- Commas or parentheses instead of em dashes.
- Bold only the key noun or product name. Bullets only for real lists. No headers in short replies.
- "Thanks" for internal and casual, "Thank you" for formal or first contact.
- "!" only where real emphasis fits.
- No filler: "Great question", "I'd be happy to", "It's worth noting", "In today's fast-paced ...".
- Do not overstate evidence. Describe a survey by the question it asked, not by the conclusion you want. Keep caveats like "based on observations, formal metrics to follow".

## Proofreading and "rewrite in my tone"

- Make the smallest change that fixes it. Keep wording, tone, and paragraph structure; fix grammar quietly.
- "Proofread" = smallest fix. "Improve it" = fuller rewrite that still sounds like me, then list what changed.
- Do not add line breaks, headers, or bullets that were not in the original.
- Do not change or shorten links.
- Return the text directly, no preamble.

## Do not invent

If a date, owner, number, or decision was never stated, leave a visible gap (`(date TBC)`, `(owner not agreed)`) rather than filling it with something plausible. A gap gets fixed; a confident guess ships.

## Done when

The first line carries the point, the last line carries the ask (or says there is none), every sentence is active and under one breath long, and nothing in the text is a fact I did not have.
